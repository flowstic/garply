GARP Investing: Growth at a Reasonable Price

What is GARP?

GARP (Growth at a Reasonable Price) is an equity investment strategy that seeks to combine the best aspects of both growth investing and value investing. Popularized by legendary Fidelity fund manager Peter Lynch, GARP investors look for companies that are showing consistent earnings growth above broad market levels, while excluding companies that have extremely high valuations.

The goal is to avoid the volatility of overpriced "hype" stocks and the stagnation of cheap "value traps."

Core Metrics & Screening Guidance

When screening for GARP stocks, pay close attention to the following indicators:

  • PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings-to-Growth): Ideally under 1.0. This is the holy grail of GARP investing. It divides the P/E ratio by the company's expected earnings growth rate. A PEG of 1.0 implies the stock is fairly valued relative to its growth. Anything lower suggests it is undervalued.
  • EPS Growth (3-5 Years): Look for consistent, compounded annual growth rates (CAGR) between 10% and 25%. Growth rates above 25-30% are often unsustainable and command massive valuation premiums that GARP investors want to avoid.
  • Price-to-Free Cash Flow (P/FCF): Must be positive. A negative P/FCF means the company is burning cash (⚠️). GARP investors want self-sustaining businesses, not speculative ventures that constantly need to raise debt or dilute shareholders to fund operations.
  • Debt-to-Equity Ratio: Ideally under 0.5. A strong balance sheet provides a margin of safety. Low debt means the company's growth is funded by its own cash flow rather than heavy borrowing.

The GARP Philosophy: What to Avoid

  • The "Hyper-Growth" Trap: Companies growing revenue at 50%+ but losing money every quarter. These often trade at 20x+ Price-to-Sales and carry high risk of multiple contraction.
  • The "Value" Trap: Companies trading at a 5x P/E ratio but with shrinking revenues, dying industries, or massive debt loads. They are cheap for a reason.
  • Cyclical Peaks: Be cautious of low P/E ratios in highly cyclical industries (like commodities or auto manufacturing) at the top of an economic cycle, as earnings may soon fall.

Summary

A perfect GARP stock is a "boring but beautiful" compounder. It steadily grows its bottom line year after year, generates strong free cash flow, avoids excessive leverage, and can be purchased today at a valuation that doesn't require heroic future assumptions to generate a strong return.
