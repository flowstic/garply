What is GARP?

GARP (Growth at a Reasonable Price) is an equity investment strategy that seeks to combine the best aspects of both growth investing and value investing. Popularized by legendary Fidelity fund manager Peter Lynch, GARP investors look for companies showing consistent earnings growth above broad market levels, while excluding companies with extremely high valuations.

The goal is to avoid the volatility of overpriced "hype" stocks and the stagnation of cheap "value traps."

Core Metrics & Screening Guidance

Our screener evaluates GARP candidates using five primary indicators:

PEG Ratio (Fwd P/E to Growth) Ideally ≤ 1.0

The holy grail of GARP investing. It divides the P/E ratio by the expected earnings growth rate. A PEG of 1.0 implies the stock is fairly valued relative to its growth. Anything lower suggests it is undervalued.

Forward P/E Ratio Ideally ≤ 17x

The Price-to-Earnings ratio indicates how much the market is willing to pay today for a dollar of expected future earnings. A lower P/E ratio relative to the market and sector averages suggests the stock is reasonably priced.

EPS Growth (3-Year) 10% - 25% CAGR

Look for consistent, compounded annual growth. Growth rates above 25-30% are often unsustainable and command massive valuation premiums that GARP investors want to avoid.

Price-to-Free Cash Flow Positive

A negative P/FCF means the company is burning cash ⚠ High Risk. GARP investors want self-sustaining businesses, not speculative ventures reliant on debt dilution.

Debt-to-Equity Ideally ≤ 0.5

A strong balance sheet provides a margin of safety. Low debt means growth is funded by internal cash flow rather than heavy borrowing.

The GARP Philosophy: What to Avoid

  • The "Hyper-Growth" Trap: Companies growing revenue rapidly but losing money every quarter. These often trade at 20x+ Price-to-Sales and carry high risk of multiple contraction.
  • The "Value" Trap: Companies trading at a 5x P/E ratio but with shrinking revenues, dying industries, or massive debt loads. They are cheap for a reason.
  • Cyclical Peaks: Be cautious of low P/E ratios in highly cyclical industries (like commodities) at the top of an economic cycle, as earnings may soon fall.

Summary

A perfect GARP stock is a "boring but beautiful" compounder. It steadily grows its bottom line year after year, generates strong free cash flow, avoids excessive leverage, and can be purchased today at a valuation that doesn't require heroic future assumptions to generate a strong return.
